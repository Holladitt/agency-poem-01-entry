At eleven I hand her the board.
Bed four, bed nine, bed twelve.
I say the names out loud — that is the rule.
A name unspoken is a body unwatched.

Bed nine's drip runs slow. I show her
where the tube kinks when the bed is raised.
If it kinks again, straighten it, then sign.
If you don't sign, the morning will not know
it ever stopped, and the stopped thing counts.

At two she calls me back. Bed four,
the one we thought was leaving, is sitting up,
asking for water. Bed twelve, the quiet one,
has stopped asking for anything at all.

I stay until the sun comes up.
I do not sign the last line of the chart.
It belongs to the hands that are still here.
Mine are the ones that have to let go.
